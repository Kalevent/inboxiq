# Ideal Customer Profile — Oliver

## Who Oliver Is

**Role**: Founder, co-founder, or head of operations at a B2B SaaS company
**Company size**: 10–50 employees
**Geography**: UK / EU primary; US secondary
**Stage**: Post-product-market fit, scaling — revenue is growing but the team has not grown proportionally
**Tech stack**: Gmail or Google Workspace (primary); some use Outlook / Microsoft 365
**Current support tools**: Shared Gmail alias, maybe Help Scout or Front, possibly Zendesk at a basic tier

---

## What Oliver's Day Looks Like

Oliver opens his inbox every morning to 20–40 new emails. A significant portion — often 30+ — are repetitive: password resets, billing questions, onboarding help, feature explanations, user management requests. Each takes 3–5 minutes to read and reply to. Across his team that is 1.5–2.5 hours per day, every day, on questions that have already been answered hundreds of times.

He is not a support person. He is a founder or operator who cannot hand this off yet because the volume does not justify a hire and the team is too small to absorb it cleanly.

He works business hours. His customers email at 9pm and on weekends. They wait.

---

## Pain Points (in order of acuity)

1. **Repetitive emails consuming founder/senior time** — the same 10 question types arriving daily, every answer composable from existing knowledge
2. **Slow response times** — team works business hours; customers and prospects don't
3. **Support volume growing faster than revenue justifies a hire** — 200→500 customers doubles load; the maths for a £35–50K hire doesn't work yet
4. **Siloed inboxes with no shared intelligence** — support@, sales@, and personal inboxes operate independently; no unified view, inconsistent responses
5. **No visibility into patterns** — handles emails reactively, cannot see that 40% of volume this month is the same onboarding problem
6. **Manual routing rules that cannot use AI context** — Gmail filters match sender/subject only; cannot route by intent, sentiment, or category
7. **No ROI measurement** — knows support emails cost time but cannot quantify it for co-founders, investors, or himself
8. **Context lost in long threads** — re-reads history before every reply on multi-day conversations

---

## What Oliver Wants

- To spend less time in his inbox without losing control of customer relationships
- Draft replies that are ready to send with a quick review — not generated from scratch
- To stay in Gmail; he does not want another dashboard to check
- Visibility into what customers are actually asking about, with action one click away
- Rules and automation that use AI context (intent, category, sentiment) — not just sender address
- Proof that it works before he trusts it to send anything autonomously
- Simple onboarding: no IT involvement, no password sharing, works in under 20 minutes

---

## Objections Oliver Will Raise

### "I do not trust AI to reply to my customers"
Legitimate. He has seen AI hallucinate. Response: draft-only is the default — AI writes, Oliver sends. Confidence threshold is adjustable. The AI is trained on his own KB and past replies, not generic internet content. Every correction trains the model.

### "We already have a system for this"
He is in Gmail or Outlook and may have Help Scout / Front / basic Zendesk. Response: InboxIQ layers on top of his existing inbox — labels and drafts appear inside Gmail, he never opens a separate tool. If he disconnects, his inbox returns to normal with nothing migrated or lost.

### "It will not understand my specific product"
Generic AI gives generic answers. Response: InboxIQ ingests his knowledge base and past replies; onboarding includes a KB setup step. He can paste 5 past email replies and see the AI use them within 10 minutes of connecting.

---

## ROI Frame That Resonates

Do **not** lead with "saves you hours." Lead with:

> "InboxIQ handles the support volume increase that would have triggered your next hire — at 3% of the cost."

- A support hire costs £35,000–50,000/year = £2,917–4,167/month
- InboxIQ at £99/month defers that hire
- Secondary frame: 80 repetitive emails/week × 4 min each = 5.3 hrs/week = 21 hrs/month saved, worth ~£525/month at £25/hr loaded cost

The business case is **deferred headcount**, not time savings.

---

## Behaviour Change Required

Oliver needs to know upfront what changes:

| What changes | Effort |
|---|---|
| Sign up with Google | Very low (2 min, no IT) |
| Connect Gmail / Outlook via OAuth | Low (2 min) |
| Upload or link knowledge base | Medium (1–2 hrs first time) |
| Review AI drafts for first 2 weeks | Low (replaces composing from scratch) |
| Adjust confidence thresholds | Low (occasional, from Settings) |

Honest message: **setup takes ~2 hours total; daily workflow is lighter than today and stays inside Gmail.**

---

## Content and Messaging Calibration

When creating content for Oliver:

- **Tone**: Peer-to-peer, direct, no fluff — Oliver is smart and time-poor, he will skip anything that feels like marketing
- **Format**: Short paragraphs, concrete numbers, real scenarios — not abstract capabilities
- **Proof**: Specific metrics beat vague claims ("4 minutes per email", "$0.001 per email in AI cost", "0.4 of a hire handled this month")
- **Angle**: He is not looking for software — he is looking for leverage. Frame InboxIQ as the leverage that makes his small team punch above their weight.
- **Journey stage calibration**:
  - *Awareness*: Lead with the pain ("30 emails a day on the same questions") — do not mention InboxIQ yet
  - *Consideration*: Show how InboxIQ solves it without disrupting Gmail — draft mode, KB grounding, stays inside his existing workflow
  - *Decision*: Concrete ROI (£99 vs £2,917/month hire), risk reversal (disconnect anytime, nothing migrated), social proof from similar-stage founders

---

## What Oliver Is Not

- Not a 200-person company with a dedicated support team — Zendesk full suite, Intercom, and Salesforce are for them
- Not an IT buyer — he does not want procurement, IT approvals, or contract negotiations
- Not looking for a chatbot for his website — he wants his *email inbox* to work better
- Not a developer — he should never need to touch code or write a regex to get value from InboxIQ
