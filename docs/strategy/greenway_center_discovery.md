## Where InboxIQ is strong

**Q1 — Who and what pain?**
Teams managing shared inboxes (support@, hr@, it@) who are drowning in manual sorting, forwarding, and drafting. The pain is real — but it's currently aimed broadly at "any team with email."

**Q6 — Market large enough?**
Yes. Every company with a shared inbox has this problem.

**Q7 — Actively looking?**
The outreach campaign achieved a 96.6% open rate (57/59 emails opened). People are curious and the subject line resonates.

**Q10/11 — What are they using and where is it failing?**
Gmail/Outlook natively (no routing, no triage), or Zendesk/Freshdesk (both handle email well but pull it into a separate platform — your team leaves their inbox and logs into a dashboard, per-seat pricing means you pay for every person who needs access, and setup takes days). The barrier is cost and migration, not capability. InboxIQ's edge — inbox-native, team operations, auto-send — is genuine and defensible.

**Q12 — Can someone understand your edge in under 10 seconds?**
Previously a fail — the hero sub-headline read "InboxIQ drafts contextual replies directly inside Gmail and Outlook..." which is a product feature, not a competitive edge. Revised hero now reads:

- *Built for customer support and operations teams.*
- *Routes, replies, and escalates automatically — without anyone prompting it. Works inside Gmail and Outlook with no new dashboard.*

ICP qualifier visible immediately. Edge visible without scrolling. **Pass.**

**Q16 — 30-day MVP?**
Already live. The product exists and is running in production.

## Where Thanh is right to push

**Q4 — Have you spoken to 20 people who experience this problem?**
This is the hard one. The outreach campaign sent 59 emails, 57 opened, 1 replied, 0 converted. That gap is a signal. People are interested but not buying. It is unknown whether the cause is wrong ICP, unclear value, or insufficient urgency to pay.

**Q2/5 — What are they currently doing and what is the moment of pain?**
There is a working theory but it has not been confirmed through enough real customer conversations yet.

**Q15 — The one job it must do better than anything else?**
This shifted during product development — from "email drafting" to "shared inbox management." That is the right direction but it is still broad. *Shared inbox for who, specifically?*

The strongest ICP signal from existing data: **founders and ops leads at B2B SaaS companies with 5–30 employees who manage a shared support@ inbox in Gmail or Outlook, spending 1–2 hours a day manually triaging — volume too high to handle comfortably, not enough to justify Zendesk's per-seat cost.**

Evidence: the outreach subject line "Before you hire a support manager" achieved 96.6% open rate against this profile. The pain is daily, they are already in Gmail/Outlook, and per-seat pricing makes traditional helpdesks unattractive.

What is not yet confirmed: whether that pain is urgent enough to pay. Zero conversions from 57 opens means either the ICP is right but the value proposition is not landing clearly enough, the friction to start is too high, or the timing is wrong. This needs more discovery calls to answer — not more features.

**Q25 — Building because users need it or because you want to build?**
Thanh is asking this directly. The honest position: strong evidence the problem exists generally, but not enough evidence that specific customers will pay for this specific solution.

## The sharper focus question

The outreach campaign targeted general B2B SaaS. The Greenway Centre is a **charity/nonprofit in Bristol** — a different ICP entirely. Before that meeting, the real question is: what does a charity's shared inbox actually look like?

Likely different problems to a SaaS company:

- Volunteer coordinators using one info@ inbox shared across 3–4 people
- No budget for Zendesk or similar tools
- Emails from funders, service users, council, and partners all mixed together
- High sensitivity to getting the wrong response to the wrong person

This might be a sharper ICP than B2B SaaS — or it might be completely wrong. That is what the discovery meeting is for.

## Questions to ask the Greenway Centre

**Ground rule: do not demo. Do not mention InboxIQ. Just listen.**

1. Walk me through what happens when an email arrives at your main inbox today — who sees it, who responds, how do you decide who handles it?
2. What types of emails arrive most often — and which ones create the most stress?
3. Has an important email ever been missed or responded to too late? What happened?
4. How many people share access to that inbox?
5. Have you ever tried any tools to manage it better — what did you try, and why did it or did it not work?
6. If you could change one thing about how your team handles email today, what would it be?
7. What does a bad week in the inbox look like?
