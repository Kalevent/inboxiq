The Problem

Your best inbound deals are going cold in your inbox

Demo requests buried under noise

A "ready to buy" email from an enterprise prospect arrives in the same inbox as newsletters, transactional receipts, and support noise. By the time it's seen, the prospect has moved on.

No urgency signal on deal intent

You can't tell from a subject line whether an email is a genuine enterprise inquiry or a low-intent casual question. Every email gets the same priority — which means no email gets the right one.

Manual scheduling kills deal momentum

A warm prospect asks for a call. Booking takes three back-and-forth emails and a Calendly link. By the time the meeting is set, the energy of the conversation has cooled.

{# ── How It Works ──────────────────────────────────────────── #}
How It Works

Every sales email triaged before your team opens it

1

Intent and deal signal classified at intake

InboxIQ reads the full email — not just the subject — and classifies intent: demo request, pricing inquiry, partnership, enterprise trial, or general question. Deal signals (company size, urgency language, buying timeframe) are scored. Enterprise inquiries are escalated immediately.

2

Draft replies from your pricing and feature docs

Sales and pricing inquiries get a KB-grounded draft reply in your Gmail or Outlook drafts. Connect your pricing page, feature documentation, or a Notion workspace — the draft answers the specific question asked, not a generic template.

3

Meeting requests booked directly from the draft

InboxIQ detects "can we have a call?" signals, pulls your Google or Outlook Calendar availability, and includes booking options in the draft. No Calendly link. No back-and-forth. One reply to confirm the meeting.
